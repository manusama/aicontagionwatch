The loop, traced

Reported money flows that circle between chipmakers, AI labs, cloud providers and their financiers.

The IMF’s October 2026 Global Financial Stability Report reviewed 14 AI financing deals: about US$300bn of equity and US$650bn of debt, tied to roughly US$1.4tn of future spending commitments flowing back to the investors. It names an AI correction as one of three ‘salient risks’, warning of a ‘triple whammy’: equity falls, debt gets harder to service, and promised revenue fails to arrive.

Downturn scenarios

How far each group could fall in three scenarios. These bands are illustrative assumptions anchored to past busts, not forecasts. The lesson from 2008: in a forced-selling phase almost everything falls together, including gold miners; what differs is margin resilience and recovery speed.

1 · Contained repricing

AI valuations reset but credit holds outside the weakest borrowers. Quality names linked to AI demand get marked down with the theme; the rest of the market rotates rather than crashes.

Trigger: Capex guidance slows; stress stays in Oracle, neoclouds and chips.

2 · Credit event

Circular commitments start failing. Forced selling hits everything with liquidity — including gold miners — before quality and havens separate from the pack.

Trigger: A neocloud default or an OpenAI funding gap; private-credit losses; Fed still hiking on oil.

3 · Global recession

Wealth effect drags consumption and investment. Base metals follow industrial demand down; gold and low-cost producers keep positive margins but their shares still fall first.

Trigger: The IMF ‘triple whammy’ plays out: equity, debt and promised revenue fall together, plus an oil shock.
