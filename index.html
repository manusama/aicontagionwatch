The loop, traced

Much of the AI build-out is paid for by the same handful of companies investing in, lending to and buying from each other. If one link fails, the others feel it.

The IMF’s October 2026 Global Financial Stability Report reviewed 14 AI financing deals: about US$300bn of equity and US$650bn of debt, tied to roughly US$1.4tn of future spending commitments flowing back to the investors. It names an AI correction as one of three ‘salient risks’, warning of a ‘triple whammy’: equity falls, debt gets harder to service, and promised revenue fails to arrive.International Monetary Fund, Global Financial Stability Report, October 2026

If it breaks: how far things could fall

Three illustrative scenarios, anchored to past busts. These are assumptions, not forecasts. In a forced-selling phase almost everything falls together, gold miners included; what differs is how well margins hold and how fast prices recover.

AI valuations reset but credit holds outside the weakest borrowers. Quality names linked to AI demand get marked down with the theme; the rest of the market rotates rather than crashes.

What would trigger it: Capex guidance slows; stress stays in Oracle, neoclouds and chips.
